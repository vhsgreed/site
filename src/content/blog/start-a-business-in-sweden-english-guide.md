Sweden does not require a general "business licence" to become self-employed.
Instead, the process is built from a handful of registrations, and the exact
set depends on which business structure is chosen. This guide is the English
entry point to that process. It is not legal or tax advice; confirm details
with [Verksamt.se](https://verksamt.se/en), the joint website of the Swedish
government agencies for business.

## Step 1: choose the business structure

Two structures cover most small businesses:

- **Enskild firma (sole trader)**: no legal distinction between the owner and
  the business. No start-up capital required. Registration with Bolagsverket
  is voluntary in most cases, but registration with the Swedish Tax Agency is
  mandatory.
- **Aktiebolag (limited company)**: a separate legal entity. The share capital
  of a private limited company must be at least SEK 25,000, and registration
  with Bolagsverket is what creates the company.

The comparison matters because liability, taxation, and administration differ:
see [enskild firma vs aktiebolag: choosing a structure](/blog/enskild-firma-vs-aktiebolag/).

Other structures exist (trading partnership, branch, economic association)
and are described on
[Bolagsverket's type of business pages](https://bolagsverket.se/en/foretag.473.html).

## Step 2: register

- Sole traders register with the Tax Agency (F-tax) and, optionally and in
  some cases mandatorily, with Bolagsverket. The step-by-step process is in
  [how to register an enskild firma](/blog/register-enskild-firma-step-by-step/).
- Limited companies are formed through a documented formation process:
  memorandum of association, paid-in share capital, a bank certificate, and
  an application to Bolagsverket. Details in
  [how to register an aktiebolag](/blog/register-aktiebolag-step-by-step/).

## Step 3: tax registrations

Most businesses need an F-tax approval (F-skatt) before invoicing, and
registration for VAT (moms) if turnover passes the threshold. Both are
covered in [how to apply for F-skatt](/blog/f-skatt-apply-enskild-firma/) and
[VAT for small businesses in Sweden](/blog/moms-vat-small-business-sweden/).

## Step 4: running obligations

Depending on structure: bookkeeping under the Bookkeeping Act, VAT returns,
income declarations (see [the Swedish small business forms guide](/blog/swedish-small-business-forms/)
and [the 2026 tax deadlines](/blog/swedish-tax-deadlines-2026/)), and, for
limited companies, annual reports filed with Bolagsverket within seven
months of the financial year end.

## What it costs

- Enskild firma: no start-up capital; Bolagsverket registration fees apply
  only for the optional business-name registration.
- Aktiebolag: at least SEK 25,000 share capital plus a registration fee
  (SEK 2,700 on the paper application route; e-service prices differ).

## Citizens of other countries

Foreign citizens can start businesses in Sweden, but some steps require a
Swedish personal identity number or co-ordination number, and the online
e-services require Swedish electronic identification. See
[starting a business in Sweden as a foreign citizen](/blog/starting-business-sweden-foreign-citizen/).
